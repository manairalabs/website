The statistics are sobering: according to Gartner, 87% of AI projects never make it into production. That's not a technology problem—it's an execution problem.

At Manaira Labs, we've seen both sides. We've inherited failed projects from other consultants, and we've shipped solutions that are still running years later. Here's what we've learned.

## The Three Killers of AI Projects

### 1. Strategy Without Execution

The most common failure mode: a consulting firm delivers a beautiful 200-page deck about AI opportunities, collects their fee, and disappears. Your team is left with recommendations but no roadmap to implementation.

**The fix:** Never separate strategy from implementation. Every recommendation should come with working code, not just PowerPoint.

### 2. Pilot Purgatory

You've probably seen this: a proof of concept that works great in the demo but never graduates to production. The pilot becomes permanent, living in a notebook somewhere, while the business problem remains unsolved.

**The fix:** Define production requirements from day one. Build for scale, not for demos.

### 3. Technology Theater

Sometimes teams fall in love with the technology itself. They build impressive systems that solve the wrong problems or create solutions looking for a problem.

**The fix:** Start with business outcomes. What metric moves if this succeeds? If you can't answer that, you're not ready to build.

## What Successful AI Projects Have in Common

After shipping dozens of AI solutions, we've identified the patterns that work:

1. **Clear success metrics** defined before any code is written
2. **Weekly demos** to stakeholders (not quarterly reviews)
3. **Production mindset** from the first commit
4. **Business ownership** alongside technical ownership
5. **Iteration speed** measured in days, not months

## The Engineering-First Approach

At Manaira Labs, we don't believe in AI consulting that stops at recommendations. Every strategy we develop comes with implementation. Every recommendation becomes working code.

Here's our typical timeline:
- **Week 1:** Discovery and success criteria definition
- **Week 2-3:** Architecture and technical design
- **Week 3-6:** Build with weekly demos
- **Week 6-8:** Production deployment and handoff

That's 8 weeks from kickoff to production. Not 8 months. Not "Phase 1 of a multi-year transformation."
